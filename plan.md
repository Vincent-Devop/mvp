# PRINCE BENJA DESIGNS — Website Implementation Plan

## Product scope and implementation

Build the approved responsive travel-retail storefront, connected business workspace and dedicated POS interface. Preserve the full approved scope: public product discovery through cart, checkout, support and order tracking; administration for catalogue, branches, stock, orders, customers, payments, suppliers, fulfilment, promotions, finance and staff; and a fast branch POS workflow with payment capture, cash-register reconciliation and shared receipts.

Use the initialized React, TypeScript, Express, tRPC and Drizzle project. The user specifically selected PostgreSQL hosted on Neon, so migrate the starter schema/query layer from MySQL to PostgreSQL and add a compatible PostgreSQL driver. Connect using a protected `NEON_DATABASE_URL` environment value. The project’s managed MySQL-compatible database was enabled during initialization and cannot be disabled; do not use its `DATABASE_URL` in the application or store business data there. Keep modules separated so products, stock, orders, payments and receipts remain extensible. Use additive, deterministic schema migrations and avoid destructive changes to existing shared data.

The customer site includes the editorial homepage; shop, category, brand and search discovery; category, price, brand, colour and size filters; sort; product detail and variants; badges; wishlist, quick view, recently viewed and comparison; cart/coupons; guest checkout; order confirmation and tracking; wholesale enquiry/registration; branch/contact/about; travel content/FAQs/reviews; and privacy, terms, delivery and returns policy pages. Product information supports SKU, KES retail/wholesale pricing, old price/discount where applicable, photos, description, variant-level SKU/stock, colours/sizes, dimensions, weight, material, specifications, warranty/estimated delivery, real reviews where verified, related products and product-specific WhatsApp enquiry. Include luggage size guidance, bundles, newsletter capture, Instagram showcase without claiming unverified account ownership, and click-to-chat to `+254768524870`.

Checkout supports delivery or branch collection, address/instructions, delivery zone/fee, sufficient-stock branch selection and reservation/collection reference; payment-method selection; and guest or account checkout. Track order placed, processing, ready for dispatch, shipped/out for delivery, delivered, failed, cancelled, refunds, returns and exchanges separately from pending/paid/failed payment state. Build the usable customer/admin/POS interface and persist the implemented product, inventory, order, payment-status, customer, branch and receipt records in Neon. Any payment status may advance to paid only from verified provider confirmation or documented authorised staff confirmation. No STK Push, bank screenshot or frontend response proves payment.

The business workspace has dashboard analytics; product/category/brand/variant/image management; branch-level inventory; stock movement, reservations and transfers; orders and returns/refunds; customers and wholesale approvals; payment reconciliation/receipts/invoices; suppliers/procurement/goods receipt; delivery zones/dispatch/collections; promotions/content/blog/reviews/newsletter; financial, sales and stock reports; staff, roles, branch access and audit history. The dedicated `/pos` page supports product/SKU/barcode search, variant and stock display, customer selection, authorised discounts, sale basket, split payments, suspend/resume/cancel, register sessions and movements, transaction history, returns/exchanges and receipt preview/print/download. Both POS and online sales use common order/sale, payment allocation, inventory movement, receipt and reporting concepts. Required permissions are enforced on the server, including branch scoping; high-risk discounts, refunds and stock changes are recorded/approved.

Branch stock distinguishes on-hand, reserved, available (`on hand − reserved`), in transit and damaged; damaged stock is not sellable. The transfer lifecycle is request, authorised approval, dispatch into transit, physical destination receipt and quantity verification, then ledger updates. Transactions/concurrency controls prevent overselling and duplicate stock deductions. Branches remain configurable; Pipeline Stage is the only confirmed location and must not be portrayed as one of several existing locations. Support retail and wholesale price tiers, wholesale approval, minimum quantities, volume breaks, quotations and approved negotiated pricing; do not grant automatic credit sales.

Use example products and figures only as visibly illustrative where actual business data has not been supplied. Do not invent verified reviews, actual sales, actual stock, branch locations, Instagram ownership, tax registration or other business facts. Automated WhatsApp Business messaging, Daraja production credentials, payment-provider accounts/callbacks, final printer/terminal arrangement, offline synchronization, and actual eTIMS obligations remain unconfigured or undecided. Make their states explicit; do not promise production payment, tax compliance, hardware support, backup/restore or availability without setup and verification. Taxes must be configurable, with no hardcoded VAT assumption. A sale receipt is distinct from a tax invoice. Register reconciliation is `opening float + cash sales − cash refunds + cash-in − cash-out`; bank/M-Pesa amounts do not count as drawer cash. An offline indicator must not misreport M-Pesa as confirmed; controlled cash queueing is not an unlimited offline promise.

## Requested checkout, catalogue and admin extension

Expand the existing `/checkout` page into an explicit payment/sandbox experience. A simulation action may write a uniquely referenced `isDemo` order and a pending payment row to Neon for an illustrative cart, but it must never contact a provider, capture funds, mark the order paid, reserve or decrement operational inventory, or issue a sale receipt. Keep such orders visibly labelled and excluded from real fulfilment, stock and financial KPIs; continue to reject sample products from normal/live checkout. Avoid inserting artificial sales merely to make reports appear populated.

Keep six customer-facing demo categories available—Suitcases & Trolleys, Travel Duffel Bags, Travel Backpacks, Handbags & Ladies' Bags, Business Bags and Travel Accessories—with at least ten unique illustrative product records each. Use unique slugs, product SKUs and variant SKUs, repeat-safe additive seeding, and the existing visibly illustrative catalogue labels. These records are not actual merchandise or verified inventory.

Extend `/admin` with a dedicated responsive sales-analytics view backed by aggregate queries over Neon orders: verified paid non-demo revenue, order totals, average order value, time series, payment status, channel, branch and best-selling product breakdowns, plus separately disclosed pending amounts. Never count a simulated/demo order or pending payment as revenue, and show truthful empty/zero states rather than generated sample sales. Expand inventory management with variant/SKU and branch filtering, low-stock review, on-hand/reserved/damaged/in-transit/available balances and server-audited adjustments; demo seed balances remain read-only.

## Project structure

- `client/src/pages/` — storefront, customer journey, admin portal and dedicated POS routes.
- `client/src/components/` — navigation, product cards, forms, dialogs, operational tables, charts and status indicators.
- `client/src/data/` — only clearly labelled demonstration content where persistent data is not yet provided.
- `client/src/lib/` — KES formatting, cart/wishlist, route metadata, typed API client and shared validators.
- `server/routers.ts` and focused feature modules — validated public/protected API operations and server-side RBAC/branch authorization.
- `server/` and `drizzle/` — Neon PostgreSQL access, additive migrations and data types/queries.
- `client/public/` and managed project storage — bespoke artwork, favicon, route manifest and crawler documents.

## Design direction

**Movement:** Modern African travel editorial: premium luggage-house presentation, now anchored by the owner-supplied PB Designs seal. **Principles:** product-led, spacious, confident, mobile-practical and honest about verification. **Palette:** royal violet/blue sampled from the actual logo, bright white, a pale lavender-tinted canvas and deep indigo-charcoal for readable contrast; reserve logo purple for signature actions, links and selected states. **Layout:** asymmetrical editorial hero followed by browseable collection bands and product grids; management uses calm data-dense tables and a persistent side rail. **Motifs:** the transparent supplied PB Designs crest, refined violet rules and concise wayfinding labels. **Interactions:** immediate, low-motion responses and clear filter/cart states. **Animation:** restrained reveal/hover transitions and reduced-motion support. **Typography:** high-contrast serif headlines and a contemporary sans-serif system, with a 16px body baseline, 13px primary navigation/controls and at least 11px for utility labels, tuned to wrap cleanly on mobile. **Brand essence:** considered travel essentials for Kenyan travellers, refined, assured and welcoming. **Voice:** direct and warm. Sample lines: “Pack well. Go further.” and “Find the bag for your next move.” **Wordmark:** use the actual supplied PB Designs crest and its “Professionals of Exceptional Design” signature rather than substituting a new PB mark. **Signature colour:** vivid royal violet-blue taken from the supplied emblem.
